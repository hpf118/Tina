MILPERSCOM Learning Support Centre · CFB Borden · Learning Engineering Cell · Role Architecture and Operating Model

Three levels of work. One operating model.

How the Team Lead (EDS 03), the Digital Learning Specialists (EDS 02) and the Instructional Designers (EDS 01) divide the work: by level of design, by product line and by deliverable. The roles differ in the level of the problem each one owns, not in how hard the work is.

Rule of thumb

The Team Lead decides what kind of thing the cell makes, and why. The EDS 02 designs how that kind of thing works and builds the first one. The EDS 01s build the next ten, faster and to the same standard. The TDO decides when, and who.

02

Why the EDS 02 / EDS 01 line has blurred

The EDS 02s are doing much of the EDS 01 work and asking what makes their role different. The TORs explain why. None of the four causes is about the people.
Cause A
The roles are split by difficulty alone

"Complex to EDS 02, simple to EDS 01" means both do the same sequence: HLD, then storyboard, then build. Only the difficulty changes. Difficulty belongs to the project, not the role, so the line moves every time a simple project turns complex or capacity runs short.

Fix. Split by the level of the problem owned. Complexity only sets how much EDS 02 time a project gets (Tiers, page 5).
Cause B
The HLD is written into the EDS 01 TOR

EDS 01 §4.a lists "High Level Design Documents" as an EDS 01 deliverable. Telling the EDS 02 to own the HLD contradicts the TOR; writing HLDs puts the EDS 02 in EDS 01 work.

Fix. Add the level above: a Solution Blueprint owned by the EDS 02. The HLD stays a course- or module-level document written inside the blueprint (page 7).
Cause C
The EDS 02 has no line authority over the EDS 01

EDS 02 §5.b: "Supervises: Nil". EDS 01 §5.a: reports to the TDO. "EDS 02 leads EDS 01" can therefore work only as design authority: setting and reviewing the design standard on a project. It cannot work as supervision.

Fix. Line management stays with the TDO. Design authority (blueprint, gate reviews, patterns) goes to the EDS 02 of the product line (page 3).
Cause D
Half of the EDS 02 TOR is not being done

Ecosystems and learner pathways (§2.a), documented AI workflows (§2.b.iv), immersive scenario logic and vendor requirements (§2.c), usability testing, the lessons-learned process (WO 3.f), cost estimates, impact statements (§4.c). No other role covers them. While the EDS 02s write module storyboards, this does not get done.

Fix. Protect EDS 02 time for system-level and first-of-its-kind work; make surge visible (page 10).
So the fix is not to rewrite the TORs
It is to make the existing TORs work by setting out four things: the levels of design, who decides what, the deliverable at each level, and a tier rule for how much EDS 02 time each project gets. A short list of TOR edits for the next revision is on page 11.

Why the golden sample is EDS 02 work

On a T2 project the EDS 02 designs and builds the first module completely (HLD, storyboard, assessment, build) to test the blueprint and set the reference the EDS 01s will match. A golden sample is a reference, not production. The second module is EDS 01 work.

The tier sets how much EDS 02 time a project gets, never what kind of work the EDS 02 does.

Two lifecycles: projects and new capabilities

Mixing these two up is the main reason the Team Lead's role and the EDS 02's role blur.
Project lifecycle
Makes a specific product. The EDS 02 architects it, the EDS 01 builds it, the TDO schedules it, the Team Lead governs it. Pages 5–7.
Capability lifecycle
Makes a new kind of product possible: the first adaptive course, the first VR scenario, the first AI-enabled workflow. The Team Lead leads, the EDS 02 builds, the EDS 01 pilots. This is the breakthrough work of the Team Lead role.

Worked example: adaptive assessment training

Adaptive assessment belongs to the e-learning family and to PL2. As a first-of-its-kind product it is T3; the next two courses are T2; once the pattern is stable, every new course is T1.
Step 1 · Team Lead · T3
Write the Capability Charter

Turn the pilot into a reusable pattern
Step 3 · EDS 01 · T2 → T1

Step 4 · Team Lead
Decide adoption on evidence; update the ID SOP
Capability Charter · Team Lead
Challenge
Members with prior experience sit through full courses; the cell has no standard way to credit what they already know.
Hypothesis
A scenario-based placement at the start of the course lets experienced members skip content they have mastered, without lowering post-test mastery.
Success measures (illustrative; the Team Lead sets targets)
  • Seat time saved by experienced learners
  • Post-test mastery no lower than on the full path
  • Placement and post-test results agree
  • Learner and instructor acceptance
  • Effort to build each additional course
Constraints
DominKnow variables and conditional navigation · SCORM reporting to the LMS · assessment policy, incl. who approves placement credit with the Training Authority · learner-data privacy · bilingual parity
Pilot
MOD 13 DMCPG-5, already designed as the Bloom-Ladder Placement storyboard v6, with a working prototype.
Element
Pattern · designed once by the EDS 02
Instance · written per course by the EDS 01
Mastery metric
Levels L0–L3 on Bloom's taxonomy; consecutive-pass (Guttman) rule; anomaly rule: a failed lower rung caps the level
—
Placement
One placement at course start; one scenario block per sub-objective, three questions climbing the ladder; no feedback during placement; about 20 minutes at most
The scenarios and items for this course

The entry slide for each section

In Gibbons's design-layer terms, the pattern fixes the control and data-management layers; the instance fills the content, message and representation layers. Surge rule

When capacity forces an EDS 02 into EDS 01-level work beyond golden samples, the TDO and the Team Lead agree it as surge, limit it in time, and show it in the SITREP. Every week of surge is a week of architecture work not done.

What the Team Lead can say to the EDS 02s

"Your role is not a senior version of the EDS 01 role. It works at a different level. The EDS 01s answer for each course being right. You answer for the whole product line working: whether the pieces add up to the performance outcome, whether the next course can be built faster than the last, and whether this year we can do things we could not do last year. When you write a storyboard, it should be the first of its kind, the golden sample, or a surge we have both agreed to and can see on the plan."
