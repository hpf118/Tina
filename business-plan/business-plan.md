## 0. Read this first: the honest assessment

Your idea has a strong core and one serious flaw. Both need to be said plainly before any plan.

**What is genuinely strong**

1. **The pedagogy is the product, not the characters.** Look at the five prototypes in `prototypes/`. The things a parent would pay for are: the child as the protagonist, inquiry-style dialogue ("Which cup will lose water faster?"), the two-level scaffolding (Level 1 Grades 2 to 3, Level 2 Grade 4), bilingual keyword cards, "Think Like a Scientist" questions, and the "Remember" summary. No competitor in personalized children's books does this. Wonderbly, Hooray Heroes and the AI story apps personalize a name and a face into a generic story. You personalize a **learning progression**. Your measurement background is a real moat: you can build assessment into the comics and show parents evidence of learning.
2. **The "grows with your child" ladder.** The same topic at two reading and reasoning levels is a subscription in disguise. Parents buy the rainbow comic at Grade 2 and the Grade 4 version two years later.
3. **A real field test.** You made these for your daughter and her friend and took them to a theme park. That is a lived use case parents will recognize.
4. **A built-in production partner.** Your husband animating the comics into video gives you a free marketing channel and a second product line.

**The serious flaw: the business depends on other people's intellectual property**

Peppa Pig (owned by Hasbro through Entertainment One), Snoopy and the Peanuts cast (Peanuts Worldwide, majority owned by WildBrain and Sony), Bluey (BBC Studios and Ludo Studio) and Canada's Wonderland (Six Flags Entertainment) are all registered trademarks and copyrighted works. Making these comics for your own daughter is fine. Selling them, putting them on a website, monetizing YouTube videos of them, or offering "pick Peppa as the character" is trademark and copyright infringement in Canada, the US, the EU and China. In practice this means:

- Payment processors and print-on-demand suppliers will refuse or cancel orders that feature these characters.
- YouTube's Content ID and Hasbro's brand-protection teams issue takedowns and channel strikes quickly. A monetized channel built on Peppa Pig will not survive.
- You cannot obtain copyright in the parts of the work that are theirs, so the "protect it internationally" goal is impossible while the characters are borrowed.
- Licensing is not a realistic option at launch. Character licences for a preschool property of this size carry minimum guarantees that usually start in the tens of thousands of dollars per territory, require style-guide approval of every page, and are granted to established publishers with distribution. It is a Year 3 or Year 4 conversation once you have sales to show.

There is a second, smaller version of the same problem. The prototypes are AI-generated art. In the United States, purely AI-generated images are not eligible for copyright; Canada has not settled the question; China's courts have gone both ways. To own what you sell, the parts that matter (character designs, scripts, curriculum, page layouts) must have clear human authorship and be documented as such.

**The fix is simpler than it looks.** Keep the mechanism and change the cast. The child stays the hero. Replace Peppa with an original companion family, Snoopy with an original dog sidekick, and Madame Gazelle with an original teacher. Your daughter did not fall in love with Peppa's copyright; she fell in love with a warm, funny, family-centred world with short episodes. Those are format choices, and you can copy the format freely. Every section below assumes you make this change.

## 1. Executive summary

**The business.** A direct-to-consumer publisher of personalized, curriculum-aligned science and social-emotional comics for children aged 5 to 10, delivered as printed hardcover books, digital comics, and a subscription "club", with an animated YouTube series as the top of the marketing funnel. The child is the protagonist. Each title exists at two or three developmental levels so the same story can be repurchased as the child grows.

**The customer.** Parents (and grandparents) of children aged 5 to 10 in Canada and the United States who want screen-time alternatives that teach something. Secondary: elementary teachers and homeschool families. Later: Chinese-speaking families in North America, then mainland China through a local partner.

**Why now.** Generative image tools make per-child personalization affordable at unit level for the first time. Parents are anxious about screen time and about early science and math. Personalized children's books are already a proven category (Wonderbly alone has sold over ten million books). Nobody has combined personalization with real curriculum design.

**The founder advantage.** A PhD in education measurement, ten years of classroom experience, and professional learning-design practice. Competitors have engineers and illustrators. You have the one thing they cannot hire easily: the ability to design a learning progression and prove it works.

**Model and targets.** Bootstrapped. Base-case Year 1 revenue about CAD 110,000 on 1,000 printed books, 1,500 digital comics and 400 subscribers; Year 3 about CAD 900,000 with a school channel and a China partner. Contribution margin per printed book about CAD 24 before marketing. Break-even in Year 1 on roughly 1,600 printed-book equivalents. Detailed model in Section 7.

**The first 90 days.** Incorporate, file trademarks (Canada, US, and China, because China is first-to-file), disclose outside activity to your employer, build an original cast with a human illustrator, convert the five prototypes to the new cast, launch a waitlist landing page, and pre-sell 50 books to validate demand before building anything automated.

## 2. Product

### 2.1 What the product is

A **personalized learning comic**: a 24 to 32 page story in which the child (name, appearance, a friend or sibling, a family pet) investigates one science phenomenon with an original cast of companions. Each title contains:

| Element | Purpose | Already in your prototypes |
|---|---|---|
| Six-panel inquiry story | Narrative hook, predict-observe-explain structure | Yes |
| Focus labels per panel ("Focus: inertia") | Concept tagging for parents and teachers | Yes |
| Keyword cards, bilingual | Vocabulary, dual-language families | Yes (English and Chinese) |
| "Remember" summary | Retrieval practice | Yes |
| "Think Like a Scientist" questions | Formative assessment | Yes (Level 2) |
| "Challenge" extension | Differentiation for advanced readers | Yes (Level 2) |
| Try-at-home experiment page | Hands-on transfer | Add |
| Parent page: what to ask, what to notice | Parent engagement and evidence of learning | Add (your measurement background) |
| QR code to the animated episode | Links the book to the YouTube series | Add |

### 2.2 Product ladder

| Tier | Product | Price (CAD) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | Animated episodes on YouTube, printable worksheets, one free digital sample comic in exchange for email | 0 | Top of funnel |
| Digital | Personalized digital comic (PDF and web reader) | 19 | Delivered in minutes; highest margin |
| Printed | Personalized hardcover, 32 pages, 21.6 by 27.9 cm | 49 plus shipping | The gift product; birthdays and holidays |
| Bundle | Three-title set in a slipcase (one subject pack) | 129 | Raises average order value |
| Subscription | "Science Detective Club": one new personalized digital comic plus experiment sheet and video every month; printed annual collection at a discount | 14.99 per month or 149 per year | Recurring revenue, retention through the leveled ladder |
| School | Classroom licence: non-personalized leveled comics, teacher guide, assessment rubric, 30 student digital copies | 199 per classroom per year | Sold to teachers and homeschool co-ops |

### 2.3 Content packs (launch order)

1. **Physics Detectives** (force, gravity, inertia, energy, circular motion). Your Wonderland set, re-cast and set in a generic "Big Park" amusement park.
2. **Earth and Water** (water cycle, weather, seasons). Your Puddle Mystery.
3. **Light and Colour** (rainbows, shadows, reflection). Your Rainbow Mystery.
4. **Math Detectives** (measurement, fractions, patterns). Extends into a second subject with the same format.
5. **Friendship and Feelings** (social-emotional learning: sharing, disagreement, big feelings). Your Bluey idea with an original cast. Note that SEL content is where personalization is most powerful, because the child sees themselves resolving a conflict.

Each pack ships with Level 1 (Grades 2 to 3) and Level 2 (Grade 4) from the start, and a Level 0 (Kindergarten to Grade 1, picture-heavy, one sentence per panel) added in Year 2 to capture your daughter's age group and younger.

### 2.4 Curriculum alignment

Map every title to three frameworks and print the codes on the parent page:

- Ontario Science and Technology Curriculum (2022), Grades 1 to 6, strands B to E.
- Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) for the US market, for example 3-PS2-1 (forces) and 4-PS4-2 (light).
- The Chinese Compulsory Education Science Curriculum Standards (2022 edition) for the China edition.

This is cheap for you to do and impossible for the AI story apps to do credibly. It is also what teachers and school buyers look for first.

### 2.5 Personalization inputs and the privacy design

Collect the minimum: child's first name, age or grade, a hair-and-skin avatar chosen from a picker, optional friend's name, optional pet. **Do not store uploaded photos.** If you offer photo-to-avatar, convert the photo to an avatar description at upload time, delete the photo immediately, and say so on the page. This single design choice removes most of your privacy exposure under PIPEDA (Canada), Quebec's Law 25, COPPA (US), GDPR-K (EU) and PIPL (China, which requires separate parental consent for any data of a child under 14).

## 4. Business model and pricing rationale

- **Printed personalized book at CAD 49** matches the category (Wonderbly sells at roughly CAD 45 to 60). Parents anchor on it as a gift, not a workbook.
- **Digital at CAD 19** is priced below the printed book to capture impulse buyers and international customers where shipping is prohibitive. It carries the highest margin.
- **Subscription at CAD 14.99 per month** is priced at the level of a streaming service, which is the direct substitute you are selling against ("less Peppa, more physics").
- **School licence at CAD 199** is inside the discretionary spending limit of most Ontario and US teachers and small enough for a principal to approve without a tender.

Revenue mix target by Year 3: 40 percent printed, 20 percent digital, 30 percent subscription, 10 percent school and licensing.

## 5. Production pipeline

The goal is a pipeline where **you design once and the system personalizes many times**, with a human checking science accuracy and art quality before anything ships.

1. **Character bible (one-time, human illustrator).** Commission an illustrator to design the original cast: the companion family (animal or human), the dog sidekick, the teacher, and the child avatar system (six skin tones, eight hairstyles, glasses, hijab, and so on). Get written assignment of copyright. This is the single most important spend in Year 1: roughly CAD 4,000 to 8,000 for a professional character sheet set.
2. **Script templates (you).** One master script per title with personalization slots: `{child}`, `{friend}`, `{pet}`, `{he/she/they}`. Two levels per title. Science reviewed by a second educator or a subject-matter friend.
3. **Art generation (AI, trained on your own cast).** Train a consistent-character model (a LoRA or equivalent reference-image pipeline) on the illustrator's sheets so the cast looks the same on every page. Generate panels for each avatar combination, or generate backgrounds once and composite avatars. Human touch-up of every printed page. Use a provider whose terms allow commercial use and do not train on your inputs.
4. **Layout automation.** HTML or InDesign templates that pull the personalized text and panels into a print-ready PDF (CMYK, bleed, 300 dpi). Your husband's animation pipeline can share the same assets.
5. **Fulfilment.** Print-on-demand with an API: Lulu Direct, Gelato, Printful, or Blurb. Gelato and Printful print locally in Canada, the US, Europe and Australia, which keeps shipping under CAD 12 and delivery under a week. Digital orders are delivered by email link within minutes.
6. **Quality gate.** Every new title: science review, reading-level check (Flesch-Kincaid and a hand read), sensitivity check of the avatar system, a physical proof copy.

Time cost per new title once the pipeline exists: about 20 to 30 hours of your time across two levels, plus CAD 500 to 1,500 in contractor and generation costs.

### 6.3 Launch sequence

1. **Waitlist (month 2).** One-page site: the story of why you built it, three sample spreads with the new cast, "Reserve your child's first mystery" with a CAD 10 deposit. Target 200 emails and 50 deposits.
2. **Founding families (month 4).** Ship the first 50 books by hand. Ask every family for a photo of the child with the book and a one-sentence quote. This becomes the website.
3. **Public launch (month 5).** Open ordering, announce on YouTube with the first three animated episodes, run PR.

5. **Subscription launch (month 9).** Offer to every existing customer first.
6. **School pilot (month 10).** Ten Ontario classrooms free in exchange for feedback and a case study; use your assessment design to show learning gains.

## 8. Legal, IP and compliance checklist

Do these in this order. Items 1 to 3 come before you sell anything.

1. **Disclose to your employer.** As a federal public servant you are bound by the Values and Ethics Code for the Public Sector and your department's code of conduct. Outside employment and business activity must be disclosed to your deputy head (through your departmental values and ethics office) before starting, and they will assess conflict of interest. Your professional learning-design work for the government may overlap in subject matter; keep the business entirely separate (no government time, equipment, or content) and document that separation. This is a real career risk if skipped and a non-issue if done properly.
2. **Retire the borrowed characters.** No Peppa, Snoopy, Bluey, Canada's Wonderland or any other third-party IP in anything public or sold. Keep the prototypes as private personal work. The existing files in `prototypes/` are in this repository as internal reference only and must not be published.
3. **Incorporate.** A federal corporation (Corporations Canada) gives you name protection across Canada and is straightforward for a future US or China structure. Both spouses as shareholders; put the IP ownership and each person's contribution in a short shareholders' agreement now, while it is easy.
4. **Trademarks.** File the brand name and logo in Canada (CIPO), the US (USPTO) and China (CNIPA) in the same month. China is a first-to-file jurisdiction: trademark squatters watch foreign brands that get traction and register their names first, then sell them back. Filing in China before you have any Chinese presence is the single most important international IP step and costs roughly CAD 1,000 to 1,500 per class through an agent. Use the Madrid Protocol from a Canadian base filing to reach both the US and China. Also check the name in the Chinese-character form you intend to use.
5. **Copyright.** Register the character bible, scripts and the first titles with CIPO and the US Copyright Office. Keep dated records of human authorship: the illustrator's contract with copyright assignment, your script drafts, and a written description of how AI tools were used and what humans changed. This documentation is what makes the work protectable and what lets you send a takedown when someone copies you.
6. **Contracts.** Work-for-hire with copyright assignment for every illustrator, animator, voice actor and developer. A licence agreement, not a sale, for any partner edition (a science centre, a theme park).
7. **Privacy.** A privacy policy written for PIPEDA and Quebec Law 25 (which has real penalties), a COPPA-compliant flow for US customers (the parent, never the child, is your customer; no accounts for children), and a data-retention rule of deleting personalization inputs 90 days after the order. If you ever offer photo upload, get a separate explicit consent and delete the photo after avatar conversion.
8. **AI provider terms.** Use image and text models on plans that grant commercial rights and do not train on your data. Keep the cast model weights (your LoRA) as a trade secret.
9. **Product safety.** Printed books for children are subject to the Canada Consumer Product Safety Act and, in the US, CPSIA (lead and phthalates in inks). Print-on-demand suppliers provide compliance statements; keep them on file.
10. **Sales tax.** Register for GST/HST immediately (you will exceed the CAD 30,000 threshold in the base case) and for US state sales tax through your platform (Shopify handles this) once you cross state thresholds.

## 9. Brand and naming

"Tina" is your daughter's name and the name in the prototypes. Since the child's name is the personalized field, the brand should not be a child's name. It should describe the promise. Working candidates, all to be checked against CIPO, USPTO and CNIPA before use:

- **Science Detective Club** (descriptive, easy for parents, weak as a trademark because it is descriptive; pair it with a distinctive cast name).
- **The Curious Crew** (distinctive, works in Chinese as 好奇小队).
- **Mystery Lab** (short, strong in search, may be crowded).

Name the cast first. A distinctive companion character name (the way "Peppa" and "Bluey" carry their brands) is more protectable than a descriptive series name, and it becomes the YouTube channel.

## 11. International and China strategy

**Years 1 and 2: North America first.** Canada and the US share language, currency proximity, print-on-demand networks and privacy norms. This is where you establish the brand, the trademarks and the sales record that every future partner will ask for.

**Year 2: the bilingual edition for the diaspora.** The English and Chinese keyword cards are already in your prototypes. A full bilingual edition (English story, Chinese on facing panels or a flip-book) sold to Chinese-Canadian and Chinese-American families through WeChat groups, Xiaohongshu and Chinese weekend schools is a low-cost expansion with a loyal customer base, and it is fully under your Canadian legal protection.

**Year 3: mainland China through a partner, not directly.** Selling directly in China requires an ICP licence for a website, a local entity, content approval for children's publications, and PIPL compliance for minors' data. The practical route is a licence deal with a Chinese children's publisher or education platform: they localize, print, distribute and handle compliance; you receive a royalty (typically 8 to 12 percent of net for print, higher for digital) and keep the IP. Your Chinese trademark filing in month 1 is what makes this deal possible and what stops the partner from becoming your competitor. Your family background and language make you the founder who can actually have these conversations.

**Character licensing later, if at all.** Once you have sales data, licensed editions with established properties become possible (established personalized publishers do carry licensed characters). Treat it as a growth option in Year 3 or later, and only for one title line, never as the foundation.
